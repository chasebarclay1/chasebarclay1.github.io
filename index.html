Work

Argos, a search and rescue quadruped

Team lead, Georgia Tech senior design, spring 2026

Disaster zones are too dangerous for people, and commercial quadrupeds cost $75K+ without being built for search and rescue. Our team built Argos: a 12 DOF quadruped with a depth camera, 360° lidar, a thermal camera that picks up body heat, and a gas sensor, all driven from a live web dashboard.

I led the 5 person team, derived the bell crank inverse kinematics solver and wrote the gait planner. When our 30 node ROS 2 stack started eating the schedule, I led the move to Node.js and Python with no ROS at all. It recovered about a week, and we drove Argos live from a browser at the Capstone Expo.

Two robot arms that play chess

Python, MuJoCo, SO-101 arms

Two SO-101 arms face each other and play chess and tic tac toe. I wrote custom inverse kinematics solvers using geometric decomposition, planned trajectories in MuJoCo simulation before running them on the hardware over serial, and coordinated handoffs between the arms with adaptive Z axis collision avoidance and mirrored coordinate transforms.

Now

I'm a controls engineer in FORTNA's LADR rotational program, and I spend most of my time on site at distribution centers across the U.S. I code emergency stop and safety logic from electrical drawings, build the Ignition HMI screens operators use, and test every device on the floor until the system runs.

The setup work was tedious, so I built TagForge: a Python tool that turns Studio 5000 tag exports into Ignition tag imports and site test checklists. It saves 2 to 3 hours of manual entry, and I trained my teammates on it.
